How Much the Environment Changes You

A short note on how research environments do not just change what you produce. They change what parts of you get exercised enough to survive.

Last updated: June 23, 2026

I used to underestimate how much the environment can reshape a person. I do not mean only in the obvious sense of network, opportunities, or prestige. I mean at the level of pace, self-image, and even what kind of effort feels natural. Some places make you more expansive: you try more things, talk more freely, recover faster from mistakes, and naturally produce more. Other places, even if objectively excellent, can narrow you. They make you more defensive, more hesitant, more internal, and over time you begin to confuse that adaptation with your personality.

My own experience made this concrete. During some periods, partly because of depression and partly because the surrounding environment did not fit what I needed then, I stopped developing in a balanced way. I could still work, and sometimes very deeply, but that depth came at the cost of lightness, curiosity, and output. In retrospect, this is why I now take environment much more seriously. It is not only a backdrop. It is a training signal. It can make certain virtues grow almost automatically, and it can also quietly starve them.

This note is intentionally personal and incomplete. I want to come back to it later with a more careful distinction between good environments in general and good environments for a specific phase of life.
