How Edge of Stability Generalizes to Mini-Batch Algorithms

What mini-batch algorithms teach us about which parts of Edge of Stability are robust, and which parts depend on the optimizer and sampling process.

Last updated: June 23, 2026

The full-batch Edge of Stability story is already subtle: training does not simply stay in the classically stable region, but tends to organize near an instability boundary. Mini-batch algorithms make this more interesting rather than less. The relevant curvature is no longer just the full-batch top Hessian eigenvalue, and the stability boundary is no longer a single deterministic threshold. Batch size, stochastic fluctuations, momentum, and the geometry of the sampled mini-batches all become part of the mechanism.

This suggests that Edge of Stability is not best understood as one universal number such as a fixed sharpness threshold. A better picture is a family of algorithm-dependent instability boundaries. The mini-batch case teaches us what survives the passage from idealized gradient descent to practical training: the tendency to self-organize near the edge remains, but the edge itself is shaped by the update rule, the noise source, and the local curvature seen by the algorithm.
