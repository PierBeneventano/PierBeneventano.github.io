When and Why You Shouldn't Trust Your Models of SGD

To what extent, and for what purposes in practice, does it make sense to consider GD+noise or other models of SGD and Adam?

Last updated: June 23, 2026

A good model of SGD is not automatically a good model for every question about SGD. This matters more than people admit. If the goal is only to capture some broad stationary behavior, or to reason about a local escape mechanism, then replacing SGD by GD plus carefully structured noise can be productive. But if the claim depends on mini-batch structure, discrete-time effects, directional curvature, optimizer state, or the way instability is approached and maintained over time, then the simplification can destroy exactly what made the original problem interesting.

The practical question is not "is GD plus noise true?" but "true enough for what?" For some purposes, it is a useful coarse description. For others, especially around Edge-of-Stability-type behavior, implicit bias, or selective effects induced by the sampling process, it can quietly move the theory away from the mechanism. Adam and related optimizers make this even sharper: once state, adaptivity, and coordinate-wise rescaling matter, a convenient surrogate may explain the wrong object while still sounding mathematically clean.
